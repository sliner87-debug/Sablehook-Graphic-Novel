PART 8

Sable walked confidently, carrying the heavy brass-and-iron mechanical heart—the Infernal Concordance Engine.
At the bottom of the stairs, the deep shadows shifted.
Kestrel lowered her massive, razor-sharp head to the cold stone floor in a gesture of absolute, terrifying loyalty.
They stood before the Foundry's core.
Sovereign Banki raised his dark gauntlet.
With casual violence, Banki's magical threads sliced effortlessly through the ancient wards, shattering the seals on the vault door like glass.
The heavy black iron doors ground open, revealing a dark, dormant, massive circular arcane receptacle embedded in the stone floor.
With absolute precision, Sable placed the heavy Infernal Concordance Engine down perfectly into the center of the dark receptacle.
Banki stepped up directly behind her, wrapping one dark-gauntleted arm intimately and possessively around her waist.
Banki's free hand extended toward the Engine.
All four magical threads aggressively wrapped around the mechanical Engine, physically crushing and suppressing the evil reddish infernal light that tried to escape its core.
The red infernal light was violently subjugated.
A massive, visible shockwave of dark, Lawful Evil Sovereign energy pulsed outward through the entire Foundry.
The Concordance Engine now hummed perfectly.
Sable turned to him, looking up with intense, terrifying devotion.
Banki smirked down at her, the dark-purple of his Psionic thread glowing faintly in his eyes.
In the heavy, dark magic glow of the newly subjugated engine, they shared an intimate, highly passionate embrace.
Far above, massive, glowing leylines—dark-purple, blue, gold, and void-black—pulsed outward from the castle's foundation.
A high-angle shot over the dark, industrial, medieval streets of Blackwater Quay.
A group of corrupt city guards in faded blue tabards look down in sheer, bowel-loosening terror as the ground beneath their boots glows with Banki's Lawful Evil magic.
Deep in the Undercity Foundry, the massive insectoid Cliff-Skitter, Kestrel, rears up.
