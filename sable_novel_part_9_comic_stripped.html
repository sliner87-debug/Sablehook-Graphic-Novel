PART 9

Banki stood at the podium—not behind it, at it.
His voice carried the Tri-Weave's motivational harmonic.
Laughter.
Lot One: the Reedsinger.
The enforcement sale was the night's darkest moment.
The violator's body sold for fifty-five thousand.
The Rinse Below was a workshop that existed in the space between crime and craft.
The guard fell first.
The Glasswright was a gnome.
He turned.
Banki read his mind.
He set one hand on the Glasswright's temple and the other on the twin-gem crystal.
The Glasswright screamed.
He composed a brief, businesslike note that he left on the workbench: NEW MANAGEMENT.
The controlled pen waited behind three thresholds.
She waited on the beam when he entered.
Null-Fang shifted slightly at his side.
With a movement so exact it felt chosen, Kestrel came down from the upper beam and leapt to the indicated perch.
Banki opened his mind, carefully, and offered not command but question.
The reply came quicker.
He drew out the crest-token cut for family seals.
Banki smiled then, a private expression unsuited to council halls.
Caelen found the first dead drop on Tuesday morning.
Aldric did not rush.
Six blocks away, Banki felt the divine surge: a warm gold pressure through the fog.
At midnight, Banki watched the heroes through borrowed eyes.
Behind him, Seraphine filled the room with light.
Maren played a line steady enough to stand on, and the altered clerks heard it.
Seraphine moved to them with both hands bright.
Aldric found one man still conscious.
Aldric did not move the blade.
The descent to the hymn-stone chamber was through a trapdoor.
The consecration hit the hymn-stones like sunlight hitting ice.
Banki entered through the legitimate entrance in human form.
Banki extended his hand with a pouch of platinum.
The man strapped into the iron chair had sold names to the Choir.
The Tri-Weave entered through the places where fear had already made doors.
Mercurial filaments slid through the psionic channel between them.
Mirell waited in the dark below Sablehook.
Seraphine had come close enough for the Stitchroom to feel her hand at the wall.
