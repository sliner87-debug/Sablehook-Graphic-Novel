PART ONE: V3 BESPOKE

Safety was the first lie they told you.

And they told it with furniture.
A heavy oak table meant you were fed. A hearth meant you were warm.
THWACK!
I was six when I learned to sleep through the knocking.
I was seven when I broke my mother's cup.
I hid the shards under the ash.

I believed broken things could be saved if you hid them where the heat lived.
KRAKKA-KOOM!
When the violence came, it did not knock.
Thud. Thud. Thud.
The heavy oak table couldn't stop a man's boot.
One part of me screamed.

The other part went glacially still.
My first lesson:
Exits before furniture.
By twelve, I was a calculation. I eliminated variables. I was a ghost in the Quay.
Until I made a mistake.
*SNATCH*
If you pull the blade, I will break your arm. Survival is just a slow death.
I offer utility.
He smelled of cold iron and mint.
He didn't teach me how to fight. He taught me the architecture of terror.
He thought he killed the child in my head.

He didn't realize she had just taken the key.
